    Privacy · Articles 13 and 14 GDPR

    Privacy notice.

    The controller is DysNet Ideell Förening, organisation number 802444-3015, at Nybodagatan 1, 171 42 Solna, Sweden. DysNet also has an office at Rue du Chantier 2, B-1000 Brussels. You can write to us at info@dysnet.org. Our data protection officer is Tobias Arndt, at dpo@dysnet.org. For your Health Data Safe account, Health Data Safe’s officer answers at dpo.contact@healthdatasafe.org.

    Version of 11 November 2026. Earlier versions stay in the public history of this site.

    01 · The site

    Reading a page reaches our host, and Google only if you accept.

    • GitHub hosts the site. GitHub Pages serves every page from the United States. GitHub receives your IP address, the time, the page you ask for and your browser’s details, and uses them to deliver the page and protect the service. DysNet receives none of these data. This rests on our legitimate interest in running a website (Article 6(1)(f)).
    • Google Analytics counts visits only if you accept. A banner asks once. If you accept, Google records the pages you view, the time, your approximate country, and your browser and device. It stores a random identifier in a cookie that lasts up to two years, so that several pages count as one visit. Google deletes the records linked to that identifier after 14 months, and only totals remain in our reports. The site refuses Google’s advertising storage and ad personalisation. If you decline, Google’s script does not load and nothing is stored. This rests on your consent (Article 6(1)(a)).
    • Your browser keeps two notes. It keeps your answer to the banner until you change it. On pages with a map, it keeps whether the information card is open or closed until you close the tab. Neither note holds an identifier, and neither reaches us.
    • Videos load only when you press play. YouTube then receives your IP address and device information under Google’s own privacy policy.
    • The home map asks the registry for its dots. On the home page, your browser fetches the registry’s public map from registry.dysnet.org, DysNet’s server at Gandi in France. The server keeps your IP address, the time and your browser in rolling technical logs. The map it returns names nobody. This rests on our legitimate interest in showing the registry on our map (Article 6(1)(f)).
    • The site has no account, no form and no advertising. Its search runs in your browser.

    02 · Email

    We keep your messages to answer them.

    Our mail runs on Zoho’s European service, and Zoho Corporation B.V. in Utrecht is our processor. We keep correspondence with no fixed end date, because families and associations often return to a question years later. We erase your correspondence when you ask. Accounting records of membership fees and donations stay for seven years after the end of the calendar year in which the financial year closed (Swedish Bookkeeping Act 1999:1078, chapter 7).

    We answer messages on the basis of our legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f)). Membership, donations and agreements rest on a contract or a legal obligation (Article 6(1)(b) and (c)). If you describe your own or your child’s condition, your message contains health data. We hold it as a non-profit body with a health aim, for our members and the people in regular contact with us, and we disclose it to nobody outside DysNet (Article 9(2)(d)).

    If you ask for an invitation to the registry, we use your email address to send it (Article 6(1)(f)). The registry counts the invitations it sends and keeps no address. Member associations never give us their members’ addresses: an association forwards our invitation to its members itself.

    03 · Registers

    The registers name professionals and institutions.

    Some pages name professionals whose details we take from public sources (Article 14).

    • The researcher register lists research teams with their institution and country, the surnames and initials of first and last authors, and their publications on our conditions. It takes them from the affiliations recorded in PubMed.
    • The bibliography lists references with their authors as published.
    • The studies page gives the contact that each study publishes.
    • The member associations page gives the contacts that each association publishes on its own site or in a public register.
    • The People page names our board and the volunteers who maintain the registers.
    • The care centres and registries pages name institutions only.

    This rests on our legitimate interest in publishing a free resource on rare conditions (Article 6(1)(f)). We publish only professional information that its holders have already made public, and nothing about anyone’s health. To have an entry corrected or removed, write to us and name it. We act within 30 days and ask for no reason. The registers are open data under CC BY 4.0, so a copy downloaded before a removal stays with whoever downloaded it.

    04 · The registry

    The registry reads only what you allow from your Health Data Safe account.

    The registry runs at registry.dysnet.org, and this section is its privacy notice. It follows the roles set out in Health Data Safe’s public compliance matrix.

    • Two controllers each answer for their own part. Health Data Safe, a Swiss non-profit foundation in Morges, controls your account under its own privacy policy. You hold the account and decide who may read it. DysNet controls what it reads from your account with your permission, and what the registry’s server keeps. Health Data Safe acts on your permissions and never on our instructions, so it is not our processor.
    • We read the answers you give the registry. These are your condition, chosen from the Orphanet list or written in your own words; the date of birth, or the expected date before a birth; your answer to each consent; and the identifier of your account. If you give them, we also read the place where the mother lived at the start of the pregnancy and your choices for the public map. If you agree to be contacted about research, we read the email address of your account. We never see your password, and we read nothing else. These are health data.
    • We use them for research on dysmelia. We count the people who live with it and look for clusters of cases that research can investigate. With your research consent, we write to you to ask you to sign back in or to present a study. A study reaches families only when a researcher asks for it, the study has regulatory approval and DysNet’s Chair agrees. Researchers never receive your address.
    • Each purpose rests on your explicit consent. This follows Article 9(2)(a) GDPR and Article 6(7) of the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection. Each consent is a separate choice, and you can withdraw any of them at any time. For a child, a parent or guardian gives the consents in an account in the child’s name. Before birth, only the mother can register the child.
    • Your answers stay in your account. Health Data Safe hosts the account where you choose when you create it, in Switzerland or the United States. The registry app runs on a server at Gandi in France, which acts as our processor. The server keeps your IP address, the time and each request in rolling technical logs, to run and protect the service (Article 6(1)(f)). To show the questions, your browser fetches public files from Health Data Safe and from GitHub, which see your IP address. When you place the pregnancy point, your browser also reaches two OpenStreetMap services, described below.
    • The app keeps a few things on your device. Two cookies keep you signed in for up to a year and are deleted when you sign out. Your browser also keeps your language and your progress through the sign-up steps. The app uses no analytics and no advertising.
    • Only our registry team reads your record. Member associations do not see records. A researcher receives data only from the people who accept that researcher’s study, and only the data the study names. We sell nothing, give nothing to insurers or employers, and offer nobody bulk access.
    • We read your answers while your permission lasts. If you withdraw it in your Health Data Safe account, we can read nothing more. Your answers stay in your account until you delete them. We stop using your address on the day you withdraw the research consent. Your account keeps a dated record of each consent. The server’s logs roll over, so the oldest entries are deleted as new ones arrive.

    How the place of the pregnancy and the public map work

    • The place of the pregnancy is optional. The registry asks where the mother lived at the start of the pregnancy, as an address, a click on a map or coordinates. We use the point to look for clusters of cases. Only our registry team sees it, and it is never published.
    • Placing the point reaches OpenStreetMap. The map comes from OpenStreetMap’s tile server, which receives your IP address, the registry’s address and the area you look at. If you type an address, OpenStreetMap’s search service receives it with your IP address. Your account saves only the point. The OpenStreetMap Foundation runs both services under its own privacy policy.
    • Appearing on the public map is a separate choice. It has its own consent: “I agree to appear on the public DysNet map”, or, for a child, “I agree that my child appears on the public DysNet map”. If you say no, you stay in the registry, and the place of the pregnancy stays unpublished either way.
    • You choose two settings before you agree. First, you choose how widely your point is blurred: from 1 to 5 km, or 10, 25 or 50 km. Second, you choose the title of your dot: your condition, roughly which limbs, or only that you live with a limb difference. A condition written in your own words always gets the last title. You see the exact words before you agree.
    • The map shows a dot inside that area. The dot never sits at your exact point. It shows the title you chose and the number of people who share that area and title. It never shows a name, an address or the exact place. The service that draws the map receives the dot alone.
    • Your dot can stand for you alone. It appears at the next update even if nobody else shares your area and title, and it then says “1 person”. Someone who knows you or the area may recognise you. A wider area and a vaguer title make that harder.
    • Anyone can see the map. It appears on the registry’s page, on our home page and in an open file that anyone may reuse. If your title names your condition, the map makes that health information public, attached to an area.
    • You can leave the map at any time. Use “Take me off the map” in the registry app, or withdraw the map access in your Health Data Safe account. The map is republished every 15 minutes, and our home page may show your dot for up to five minutes more. Copies made before you left stay with whoever made them. Leaving does not affect the lawfulness of what happened before (Article 7(3) GDPR).
    • Both rest on your explicit consent. The place rests on your agreement to be counted, and the map on its own consent (Article 9(2)(a) GDPR; Article 6(7) of the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection). For a child, a parent or guardian decides in the child’s account.

    05 · Your rights

    You can see, correct, export or delete your data.

    Articles 15 to 21 GDPR let you ask us to:

    • give you a copy of what we hold about you, or correct it;
    • erase it, or stop using it while a question about it is open;
    • hand it over in a form you can take elsewhere;
    • stop any processing that rests on our legitimate interest.

    In the registry, you exercise these rights yourself. In your Health Data Safe account, you review, correct, export and delete your data, and withdraw any consent. The registry app also lets you download what you entered in it, as a JSON file.

    For any request, write to info@dysnet.org or to our data protection officer at dpo@dysnet.org. We answer within one month, free of charge. If a request is complex, we tell you within that month that we need more time (Article 12(3)). We take no automated decisions about anybody and build no profiles.

    Four companies process data for us. GitHub hosts this site from the United States and states that it complies with the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. Zoho carries our mail on its European service. Google counts the visits of readers who accept the banner, from the United States, and states that it complies with the same framework. Gandi runs the registry’s server in France. Health Data Safe holds your account as a controller in its own right.

    You may complain to the supervisory authority where you live, where you work or where the problem happened (Article 77), and you may go to court (Article 79). Our authority is Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten (IMY), the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection, at Box 8114, 104 20 Stockholm, imy@imy.se or +46 8 657 61 00. IMY takes complaints through its own form. In Switzerland, you may turn to the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner.
