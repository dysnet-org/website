    Care centres for limb difference: where expertise lives.

    The map of reference and competence centres for limb difference, in Europe and beyond, validated with our member associations so a family anywhere knows where the nearest expertise is. Every centre listed here was named by one of our member associations on its own website or visited by the board, and appears as an orange marker on the world map on our home page. Where DysNet has no member association yet, a centre earns its place differently: its own institutional page must state congenital limb difference, limb reconstruction or prosthetic fitting in its scope, and those entries say verified from rather than named by, so you can see at a glance which are community-validated and which are not. The dedicated children’s hand clinics of the United Kingdom and Ireland come from the directory that the British Society for Surgery of the Hand publishes for families, which our member association Reach points parents to when they ask for a referral. 95 centres in 18 countries so far; associations add theirs by writing to info@dysnet.org. Download the data (JSON, CC BY 4.0).

    Where the list comes from

    ERN BOND network centres source

    The European Reference Network for rare bone diseases connects expert hospitals across the EU. DysNet’s seat in its patient advocacy group is the channel for validating limb-difference centres.

    EU-wide · ernbond.eu

    Orphanet directory of expert centres source

    Orphanet maintains the European directory of expert centres for rare diseases, searchable by condition and country. Our register cross-references it: each DysNet-validated centre links to its Orphanet record.

    Orphanet · orpha.net/en/expert-centres

    China 6

    Beijing Jishuitan Hospital, Department of Hand Surgery Expert clinic

    北京积水潭医院 手外科

    China's founding hand-surgery department (1958, Wang Shuhuan, "father of Chinese hand surgery"). Runs a named specialist clinic for congenital hand and upper-limb deformity (先天性手及上肢畸形专病门诊) at the Xinjiekou campus and a hand and upper-limb deformity clinic at the Huilongguan campus, listed in the department's own published clinic timetable.

    Beijing, China · jst-hosp.com.cn · verified from the department's own clinic timetable

    China Rehabilitation Research Center, Beijing Boai Hospital Prosthetics & rehabilitation centre

    中国康复研究中心 北京博爱医院

    National rehabilitation institution of the China Disabled Persons' Federation, founded 1988: 1,100 beds, a department of prosthetics and a rehabilitation engineering institute, with paediatric rehabilitation among its main services. The reference point for prosthetic and orthotic fitting under the national scheme of assistive-device grants for children.

    Beijing, China · crrc.com.cn · verified from the centre's own English presentation

    National Rehabilitation Hospital, bone trauma and orthopaedic surgery Hospital reference centre

    国家康复辅具研究中心附属康复医院 骨创伤与矫形外科（骨一科）

    Hospital of the National Research Center for Rehabilitation Technical Aids (Ministry of Civil Affairs). Its orthopaedic department states congenital limb deformity, limb-deficiency repair and reconstruction, external-fixator deformity correction and the design and fabrication of orthoses as its scope, and runs a surgical assistance scheme for people with limb disability.

    Beijing, China · gjkfyy.mca.gov.cn · verified from the hospital's own department page (mca.gov.cn)

    Guangzhou Women and Children's Medical Center, department of paediatric orthopaedics Hospital reference centre

    广州市妇女儿童医疗中心 骨科

    Paediatric orthopaedic department with a hand-surgery team, within the Guangdong provincial clinical research centre for child health. Keeps a hand registry: its published series covers 2,300 thumbs in 2,108 children with congenital thumb duplication treated between 2015 and 2021, the largest congenital-hand cohort we have found in China and the most credible registry counterpart there.

    Guangzhou, China · pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov · verified from its published hand-registry series in Frontiers in Pediatrics

    Huashan Hospital, Fudan University, Department of Hand Surgery Expert clinic

    复旦大学附属华山医院 手外科

    Independent department since 1984; Shanghai clinical medical centre and national key discipline since 2002, and a training centre of the International Federation of Societies for Surgery of the Hand. Eight clinical teams over 111 beds; upper-limb deformity, and thumb and finger reconstruction by toe transfer, are among its stated fields.

    Shanghai, China · huashan.org.cn · verified from the hospital's own department page

    The University of Hong Kong-Shenzhen Hospital, division of paediatric orthopaedics Hospital reference centre

    香港大学深圳医院 小儿骨科

    Multidisciplinary paediatric orthopaedic team (orthopaedics with rehabilitation, paediatrics, obstetrics, neurosurgery, anaesthesiology and medical social work) naming congenital hand deformities and polydactyly among its clinical scope. Set up the first multidisciplinary team in China for osteogenesis imperfecta and now a skeletal-dysplasia centre. Hong Kong governance and an English-language site, the most accessible first contact for a foreign organisation.

    Shenzhen, China · hku-szh.org · verified from the hospital's own division page

    Ethiopia 1

    India 6

    Freedom Trust Prosthetics & rehabilitation centre

    Founded in 1997 by Dr Sunder Subramaniam, a rehabilitation specialist, to take prosthetics and orthotics to rural India through mobile camps. Its Walk India project fits amputees in villages with custom prostheses, Gram Vikalang Punarjanmam supplies callipers, wheelchairs and crutches, and the trust states that it supports people from paediatric to geriatric care. Like the other Indian fitting charities its work is mainly with acquired limb loss.

    Chennai, India · freedomtrustchennai.org · verified from the trust's own site

    Ganga Hospital, Department of Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery Expert clinic

    Runs a named Congenital Hand Differences unit inside the plastic surgery department led by Dr S Raja Sabapathy, with four listed surgeons, a paediatric anaesthesia team, physiotherapists and social workers. Its published series cover symbrachydactyly, radial longitudinal deficiency and radial polydactyly, constriction ring syndrome, split hand and foot malformation and macrodactyly. The unit publishes Ganga Little Hands, a bulletin written for the families of children with hand differences, and its surgeons take part in the congenital-hand programmes of the IFSSH and the Asian Pacific federation.

    Coimbatore, India · gangahospital.com · verified from the department's own unit page

    Amrita Hospital Faridabad, Paediatric Orthopaedics and Deformity Correction Hospital reference centre

    Department dedicated to deformity correction and limb reconstruction in children, which it describes as the first of its kind in northern India. It names tibial hemimelia, fibular hemimelia, congenital pseudarthrosis of the tibia and upper-limb deficiencies in its scope, and runs limb lengthening, growth modulation and software-guided correction with hexapod and Ilizarov frames, alongside dedicated clinics for deformity correction and limb reconstruction, cerebral palsy and clubfoot.

    Faridabad, India · amritahospitals.org · verified from the hospital's own department page

    Bhagwan Mahaveer Viklang Sahayata Samiti (Jaipur Foot) Prosthetics & rehabilitation centre

    The organisation behind the Jaipur Foot. It states that every limb, calliper, wheelchair, tricycle and crutch is provided free of charge, that it has reached more than 2.5 million beneficiaries, and that it has held 111 fitment camps in 44 countries, with branches in Mumbai, Indore, Ranchi and Kanpur. Its stated population is amputees and people disabled by polio rather than children born with a limb difference: it is listed here as the largest prosthetic fitting capacity in the country, not as a congenital centre.

    Jaipur, India · jaipurfoot.org · verified from the organisation's own site

    National Institute for Locomotor Disabilities (Divyangjan) Prosthetics & rehabilitation centre

    The national institute for locomotor disability of the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment, founded in 1978 as the National Institute for the Orthopaedically Handicapped. Departments of prosthetics and orthotics, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, rehabilitation engineering and medical rehabilitation on one campus at Bonhooghly, with outpatient and inpatient services, disability evaluation, and the degree courses that train the country's prosthetists and orthotists.

    Kolkata, India · nild.nic.in · verified from the institute's own site

    Trishla Orthopedic Clinic and Rehab Centre Expert clinic

    Paediatric orthopaedic practice of Dr Jitendra Kumar Jain, who also chairs the Trishla Foundation. Its pages on congenital limb deficiency name proximal femoral focal deficiency, tibial hemimelia, fibular hemimelia and radial club hand, and it offers video consultation, which counts in a country where the nearest paediatric orthopaedic surgeon can be a day of travel away.

    Prayagraj, India · trishlaortho.com · verified from the clinic's own condition pages

    Zambia 1

    Beit CURE Hospital (CURE Zambia) Paediatric orthopaedic teaching hospital

    Children's teaching hospital of the CURE network. Its own pages give the conditions it operates on, among them clubfoot, bowed legs, knock knees, windswept legs, osteogenesis imperfecta and burn contractures, and the network states that after surgery children receive physical therapy and, where needed, orthoses and prosthetic limbs. Around 3,000 reconstructive, orthopaedic and ENT operations a year for children, in a teaching hospital of six buildings with 57 beds and three operating rooms.

    Lusaka, Zambia · cure.org · verified from the hospital's own page

    Zimbabwe 1

    CURE Children's Hospital Zimbabwe Paediatric orthopaedic teaching hospital

    Children's teaching hospital of the CURE network. Its own pages give the conditions it operates on, among them clubfoot, bowed legs, knock knees, windswept legs, osteogenesis imperfecta and burn contractures, and the network states that after surgery children receive physical therapy and, where needed, orthoses and prosthetic limbs. The country's first and only charitable children's hospital for orthopaedic disabilities such as clubfoot, bowed legs and knock knees, with 41 beds and three operating rooms, and holder of SafeCare level 5 accreditation.

    Bulawayo, Zimbabwe · cure.org · verified from the hospital's own page
